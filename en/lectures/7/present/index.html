Lecture 7

Angular momentum: the multiplets of spin jjj

Rotations about different axes do not commute. When they act on a system by unitaries that do not commute either, Lecture 6 shows that a Hamiltonian unchanged by them has degenerate levels. This lecture finds which degeneracies can occur. An angular momentum is a triple of observables with the commutation relations of the spin of Lecture 1. We prove that every angular momentum on Cn\C^nCn splits into multiplets of spin jjj, of dimension 2j+12j+12j+1, and that a multiplet is fixed by jjj alone. The three-level system of Lecture 4 turns out to be a multiplet of spin 111. If the Hamiltonian commutes with time reversal, every level of a system of half-integer spin is evenly degenerate.

7.1 Angular momentum

The spin of Lecture 1 is the triple Sa=ℏ2σaS_a=\tfrac\hbar2\sigma_aSa​=2ℏ​σa​. By the Pauli rule, σxσy−σyσx=2iσz\sigma_x\sigma_y-\sigma_y\sigma_x=2\ii\sigma_zσx​σy​−σy​σx​=2iσz​, so SxSy−SySx=iℏSzS_xS_y-S_yS_x=\ii\hbar S_zSx​Sy​−Sy​Sx​=iℏSz​, and the same holds for the cyclic permutations (x,y,z)→(y,z,x)→(z,x,y)(x,y,z)\to(y,z,x)\to(z,x,y)(x,y,z)→(y,z,x)→(z,x,y). These relations come from rotations. By Theorem 1.12, the unitary e−iθ n⋅σ/2=e−iθ n⋅S/ℏe^{-\ii\theta\,\nvec\cdot\sigvec/2}= e^{-\ii\theta\,\nvec\cdot\mathbf S/\hbar}e−iθn⋅σ/2=e−iθn⋅S/ℏ turns the Bloch sphere by the angle θ\thetaθ about n\nvecn. Rotations about different axes do not commute, and neither do their generators. For a system larger than a qubit, the observables that generate its rotations in the same way are its angular momentum, and they must obey the same relations. We take the relations as the definition. Exercise Sheet 7 shows that they make e−iθJz/ℏe^{-\ii\theta J_z/\hbar}e−iθJz​/ℏ turn the pair (Jx,Jy)(J_x,J_y)(Jx​,Jy​) through the angle θ\thetaθ.

Definition 7.1 (Angular momentum). An angular momentum on Cn\C^nCn is a triple of self-adjoint matrices JxJ_xJx​, JyJ_yJy​, JzJ_zJz​ with [Jx,Jy]=iℏJz,[Jy,Jz]=iℏJx,[Jz,Jx]=iℏJy.\begin{equation} \label{eq:7:comm} \comm{J_x}{J_y}=\ii\hbar J_z ,\qquad \comm{J_y}{J_z}=\ii\hbar J_x ,\qquad \comm{J_z}{J_x}=\ii\hbar J_y . \end{equation}[Jx​,Jy​]=iℏJz​,[Jy​,Jz​]=iℏJx​,[Jz​,Jx​]=iℏJy​.(7.1)

The spin of Lecture 1 is an angular momentum on C2\C^2C2. So is the zero triple on any Cn\C^nCn. Consider atoms whose magnetic moment is γJ\gamma\mathbf JγJ, for an angular momentum J\mathbf JJ and a number γ≠0\gamma\ne0γ=0. A Stern–Gerlach magnet along zzz reads JzJ_zJz​ on them. Silver gave two beams. The theorem of this lecture says which other numbers of beams are possible.

The Hamiltonian H=(ε/ℏ2)Jz2H=(\varepsilon/\hbar^2)J_z^2H=(ε/ℏ2)Jz2​ of the three-level system acts on a single multiplet of spin 111, yet it has two levels, 000 and ε\varepsilonε. Of the three JaJ_aJa​, it commutes with JzJ_zJz​ only. The degeneracy of its level ε\varepsilonε comes from the rotations about the zzz axis together with the mirror (Lecture 6), not from the full set of rotations.

When every rotation is a symmetry, as for an atom in no field, each level contains a whole multiplet, so a level that contains a multiplet of spin jjj is at least (2j+1)(2j+1)(2j+1)-fold degenerate. A field along zzz breaks the rotations about xxx and yyy, and it can split the level.

The electron, the proton and the neutron each have spin 12\tfrac1221​. The deuteron, the nucleus of heavy hydrogen, and the nucleus of nitrogen-14 have spin 111, and so has the pair of unpaired electrons in an NV−^-− centre, the three-level system of Lecture 4. In Stern and Gerlach’s experiment the electronic angular momentum of a silver atom is that of one electron, spin 12\tfrac1221​, and the beam splits in two. An ideal magnet for a single multiplet of spin jjj, whose magnetic moment is γJ\gamma\mathbf JγJ with γ≠0\gamma\ne0γ=0, separates up to 2j+12j+12j+1 beams, one for each reading of JzJ_zJz​, and an unpolarised beam fills all of them. Chromium ions in ruby, Cr3+^{3+}3+, have spin 32\tfrac3223​. The crystal splits their lowest multiplet into two Kramers pairs (§7.6) about 11.5 GHz11.5\ \mathrm{GHz}11.5 GHz apart. These levels, split further by an applied field, are used in ruby masers.

7.6 Time reversal

Run a motion backwards and every spin turns round. This reversal is not a unitary matrix. A map Θ\ThetaΘ of Cn\C^nCn onto itself is antiunitary if it is antilinear and reverses the order in inner products, that is, if for all vectors ϕ\phiϕ, ψ\psiψ and all numbers α\alphaα, β\betaβ Θ(αϕ+βψ)=αˉ Θϕ+βˉ Θψand⟨Θϕ,Θψ⟩=⟨ψ,ϕ⟩.\Theta(\alpha\phi+\beta\psi)=\bar\alpha\,\Theta\phi+\bar\beta\,\Theta\psi \quad\text{and}\quad \inner{\Theta\phi}{\Theta\psi}=\inner\psi\phi .Θ(αϕ+βψ)=αˉΘϕ+βˉ​Θψand⟨Θϕ,Θψ⟩=⟨ψ,ϕ⟩. Let CCC conjugate the coordinates in an orthonormal basis. The product U=ΘCU=\Theta CU=ΘC of two antilinear maps is linear, and it keeps inner products, so it is unitary. Since C2=11C^2=\IdC2=11, we have Θ=UC\Theta=UCΘ=UC. Since CU=UˉCCU=\bar UCCU=UˉC, it follows that Θ2=UCUC=UUˉ\Theta^2=UCUC=U\bar UΘ2=UCUC=UUˉ, where Uˉ\bar UUˉ has the conjugate entries. If ΘH=HΘ\Theta H=H\ThetaΘH=HΘ, then Θ\ThetaΘ maps each eigenspace of HHH into itself. Indeed, if Hψ=λψH\psi=\lambda\psiHψ=λψ, then HΘψ=ΘHψ=Θ(λψ)=λΘψH\Theta\psi=\Theta H\psi=\Theta(\lambda\psi)=\lambda\Theta\psiHΘψ=ΘHψ=Θ(λψ)=λΘψ, since λ\lambdaλ is real.

Theorem 7.8 (Kramers’ degeneracy). Let Θ\ThetaΘ be antiunitary with Θ2=−11\Theta^2=-\IdΘ2=−11, and let Θ\ThetaΘ commute with a self-adjoint HHH. Then every eigenspace of HHH has even dimension.

Proof. By antiunitarity, ⟨ψ,Θψ⟩=⟨Θ2ψ,Θψ⟩=−⟨ψ,Θψ⟩,\inner\psi{\Theta\psi}=\inner{\Theta^2\psi}{\Theta\psi}=-\inner\psi{\Theta\psi} ,⟨ψ,Θψ⟩=⟨Θ2ψ,Θψ⟩=−⟨ψ,Θψ⟩, so Θψ⊥ψ\Theta\psi\perp\psiΘψ⊥ψ. If ϕ∈V\phi\in\mathcal Vϕ∈V is orthogonal to both, then so is Θϕ\Theta\phiΘϕ. Indeed, ⟨Θϕ,Θψ1⟩=⟨ψ1,ϕ⟩=0\inner{\Theta\phi}{\Theta\psi_1}=\inner{\psi_1}\phi=0⟨Θϕ,Θψ1​⟩=⟨ψ1​,ϕ⟩=0, and, since ψ1=Θ(−Θψ1)\psi_1=\Theta(-\Theta\psi_1)ψ1​=Θ(−Θψ1​), ⟨Θϕ,ψ1⟩=−⟨Θψ1,ϕ⟩=0\inner{\Theta\phi}{\psi_1}=-\inner{\Theta\psi_1}\phi=0⟨Θϕ,ψ1​⟩=−⟨Θψ1​,ϕ⟩=0. So Θ\ThetaΘ maps the part of V\mathcal VV orthogonal to ψ1\psi_1ψ1​ and Θψ1\Theta\psi_1Θψ1​ into itself. Repeat the argument there, two dimensions at a time. 

On a sum of multiplets that all have half-integer spin, Θ2=−11\Theta^2=-\IdΘ2=−11, so a Hamiltonian that commutes with Θ\ThetaΘ has every level evenly degenerate. On a single spin 12\tfrac1221​, such a Hamiltonian is therefore a multiple of 11\Id11. For integer spin Θ2=11\Theta^2=\IdΘ2=11, and Theorem 7.8 does not apply. For example, the strain ηX\eta XηX commutes with Θ\ThetaΘ and splits the level of e+e_+e+​ and e−e_-e−​ (Example 6.6). A magnetic field breaks time reversal and can split a Kramers pair, as in Lecture 1. Since it is antilinear, Θ\ThetaΘ is not a symmetry in the sense of Lecture 6 and has no generator.
