Lecture 6

Symmetry, conservation and degeneracy

What this lecture does

The three-level system of Lecture 4 has a degenerate level, and Lecture 5 found two incompatible ways to split it. Why are levels degenerate at all? This lecture answers with symmetries. An observable that commutes with the Hamiltonian keeps its statistics for ever, and a unitary that commutes with it maps every motion to another motion. Two such unitaries that do not commute with each other force a degenerate level. A magnetic field breaks one symmetry of the three-level system and splits its level in two. Finally, the first-order shifts under a small perturbation are the eigenvalues of the perturbation inside the level, up to an error that the lecture bounds.

6.1 Conserved quantities

Throughout this lecture the Hamiltonian HHH is constant, and ρt=UtρUt†\rho_t=U_t\rho\adj{U_t}ρt​=Ut​ρUt†​ with Ut=e−itH/ℏU_t=e^{-\ii tH/\hbar}Ut​=e−itH/ℏ. No measurement is made between the times 000 and ttt.

Definition 6.1 (Conserved observable). An observable A=∑aa EaA=\sum_aa\,E_aA=∑a​aEa​ is conserved if, for every density matrix ρ\rhoρ, every reading aaa and every time ttt, Tr⁡(ρtEa)=Tr⁡(ρEa).\Tr(\rho_tE_a)=\Tr(\rho E_a) .Tr(ρt​Ea​)=Tr(ρEa​).

So the probability of each reading of a conserved observable never changes, whatever the state. By Theorem 4.4, the energy is conserved.

Theorem 6.2 (Conservation is commutation with HHH). An observable AAA is conserved if and only if [A,H]=0\comm AH=0[A,H]=0.

Proof. First, let AH=HAAH=HAAH=HA. By Lemma 4.3, applied to HHH, every EkE_kEk​ commutes with AAA. Applied again, to AAA and EkE_kEk​, the lemma shows that every EaE_aEa​ commutes with every EkE_kEk​, and so with Ut=∑ke−itεk/ℏEkU_t=\sum_ke^{-\ii t\varepsilon_k/\hbar}E_kUt​=∑k​e−itεk​/ℏEk​. By the cyclicity of the trace, Tr⁡(UtρUt†Ea)=Tr⁡(ρUt†EaUt)=Tr⁡(ρEa).\Tr(U_t\rho\adj{U_t}E_a)=\Tr(\rho\adj{U_t}E_aU_t)=\Tr(\rho E_a).Tr(Ut​ρUt†​Ea​)=Tr(ρUt†​Ea​Ut​)=Tr(ρEa​). Conversely, let AAA be conserved. Then Tr⁡(ρ(Ut†EaUt−Ea))=0\Tr\bigl(\rho(\adj{U_t}E_aU_t-E_a)\bigr)=0Tr(ρ(Ut†​Ea​Ut​−Ea​))=0 for every ρ\rhoρ, and in particular ⟨ψ,(Ut†EaUt−Ea)ψ⟩=0\inner\psi{(\adj{U_t}E_aU_t-E_a)\psi}=0⟨ψ,(Ut†​Ea​Ut​−Ea​)ψ⟩=0 for every unit vector ψ\psiψ. The matrix in brackets is self-adjoint, so it is 000, as in the proof of Theorem 5.2. Hence Ut†EaUt=Ea\adj{U_t}E_aU_t=E_aUt†​Ea​Ut​=Ea​ for every ttt. The derivative at t=0t=0t=0 is iℏ(HEa−EaH)=0\tfrac\ii\hbar(HE_a-E_aH)=0ℏi​(HEa​−Ea​H)=0, so every EaE_aEa​ commutes with HHH, and so does A=∑aa EaA=\sum_aa\,E_aA=∑a​aEa​. 

By Theorem 5.2, a conserved observable is one that is compatible with the Hamiltonian. Its readings also survive the evolution. If a reading of AAA gives aaa at time 000, a second reading at time ttt gives aaa again with certainty, since the branch operator of the readings aaa and a′a'a′ is Ea′UtEa=UtEa′Ea=0E_{a'}U_tE_a=U_tE_{a'}E_a=0Ea′​Ut​Ea​=Ut​Ea′​Ea​=0 for a′≠aa'\ne aa′=a.

By the definition, a conserved observable has a constant mean Tr⁡(ρtA)\Tr(\rho_tA)Tr(ρt​A). The converse fails. A constant mean in one state, or even constant statistics in one state, does not make AAA conserved, since the definition asks for every state. Every observable has constant statistics in a stationary state.

6.2 Symmetries

Definition 6.3 (Symmetry). A symmetry of the Hamiltonian HHH is a unitary matrix VVV with VH=HVVH=HVVH=HV.

By Lemma 4.3 and (4.3), a symmetry commutes with every UtU_tUt​. Conversely, a unitary that commutes with every UtU_tUt​ commutes with HHH, as the derivative at t=0t=0t=0 shows. So VVV is a symmetry if and only if VUt=UtVVU_t=U_tVVUt​=Ut​V for all ttt. In other words, if ψt\psi_tψt​ is a motion of the system, that is, a solution of the Schrödinger equation, then so is VψtV\psi_tVψt​. Preparing VψV\psiVψ and waiting for the time ttt gives UtVψ=VUtψU_tV\psi=VU_t\psiUt​Vψ=VUt​ψ, the image under VVV of what ψ\psiψ becomes. A symmetry also keeps the energy statistics, since Tr⁡(VρV†Ek)=Tr⁡(ρV†EkV)=Tr⁡(ρEk)\Tr(V\rho\adj VE_k)=\Tr(\rho\adj VE_kV)=\Tr(\rho E_k)Tr(VρV†Ek​)=Tr(ρV†Ek​V)=Tr(ρEk​).

Symmetries often come in families, such as rotations by every angle about one axis. The next proposition links such a family to a conserved observable.

Proposition 6.4 (A family of symmetries and its generator). Let AAA be an observable, and Vs=e−isA/ℏV_s=e^{-\ii sA/\hbar}Vs​=e−isA/ℏ for s∈Rs\in\Rs∈R.

  1. All VsV_sVs​ are symmetries of HHH if and only if [A,H]=0\comm AH=0[A,H]=0, that is, if and only if AAA is conserved.

  2. A symmetry VVV with V2=11V^2=\IdV2=11 is self-adjoint, its readings are among +1+1+1 and −1-1−1, and it is conserved.

Proof.

  1. If [A,H]=0\comm AH=0[A,H]=0, then by Lemma 4.3 HHH commutes with every projection EaE_aEa​ of AAA, and so with Vs=∑ae−isa/ℏEaV_s=\sum_ae^{-\ii sa/\hbar}E_aVs​=∑a​e−isa/ℏEa​. Conversely, if VsH=HVsV_sH=HV_sVs​H=HVs​ for every sss, the derivative at s=0s=0s=0 gives −iℏ(AH−HA)=0-\tfrac\ii\hbar(AH-HA)=0−ℏi​(AH−HA)=0. The rest is Theorem 6.2.

  2. A unitary VVV with V2=11V^2=\IdV2=11 has V†=V−1=V\adj V=V^{-1}=VV†=V−1=V. Each eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ of VVV satisfies λ2=1\lambda^2=1λ2=1. Since VVV commutes with HHH, it is conserved by Theorem 6.2.

 

The observable AAA is called the generator of the family VsV_sVs​. By Proposition 6.4, the generator of a family of symmetries is conserved. In classical mechanics the corresponding statement is Noether’s theorem. We do not need it.

6.3 Symmetry forces degeneracy

Theorem 6.5 (Non-commuting symmetries force degeneracy). If a Hamiltonian HHH on Cn\C^nCn has two symmetries VVV and WWW with VW≠WVVW\ne WVVW=WV, then some energy level of HHH is degenerate.

Proof. Suppose every level of HHH is simple, with unit eigenvectors u1,…,unu_1,\dots,u_nu1​,…,un​. By the proof of Lemma 4.3, VVV maps each eigenspace of HHH into itself. Each eigenspace is the line through some uku_kuk​, so Vuk=vkukVu_k=v_ku_kVuk​=vk​uk​ for a number vkv_kvk​. In the same way Wuk=wkukWu_k=w_ku_kWuk​=wk​uk​. Then VWuk=vkwkuk=WVukVWu_k=v_kw_ku_k=WVu_kVWuk​=vk​wk​uk​=WVuk​ for every kkk, so VW=WVVW=WVVW=WV, against the hypothesis. 

The theorem predicts a degeneracy from the symmetries alone, before the spectrum is computed. One symmetry, or several that commute with one another, forces nothing. For example, §6.4 gives a Hamiltonian with a whole family of symmetries and three simple levels. Conversely, every Hamiltonian with a degenerate level has non-commuting symmetries. Inside a degenerate eigenspace, any two non-commuting unitaries of that eigenspace, extended by 11\Id11 on the other eigenspaces, are such symmetries. So the theorem is useful when the symmetries are known first, as they are when they come from the geometry of the system.

6.7 Symmetries of a ring

Lecture 8 studies a particle that hops between N≥3N\ge3N≥3 sites on a ring. Its states are the rays of CN\C^NCN, with an orthonormal basis exe_xex​, one for each site, labelled by x∈ZNx\in\Z_Nx∈ZN​, so that ex+N=exe_{x+N}=e_xex+N​=ex​. Two unitaries describe the geometry of the ring. The shift TTT moves the particle one site forward, and the mirror MMM reflects the ring: Tex=ex+1,Mex=e−x.Te_x=e_{x+1},\qquad Me_x=e_{-x} .Tex​=ex+1​,Mex​=e−x​. Symmetries of an nnn-level system

Conserved: an observable AAA keeps its statistics for every state and time if and only if [A,H]=0\comm AH=0[A,H]=0 (Theorem 6.2). Symmetry: a unitary VVV with VH=HVVH=HVVH=HV. It maps motions to motions. The generator of a family of symmetries is conserved (Proposition 6.4). Degeneracy: two symmetries that do not commute force a degenerate level (Theorem 6.5). A perturbation that breaks one of them may split it. Perturbation: an H1H_1H1​ with ∥H1∥≤g/4\norm{H_1}\le g/4∥H1​∥≤g/4 shifts a level ε\varepsilonε by the eigenvalues of EH1EEH_1EEH1​E, up to 3∥H1∥2/g3\norm{H_1}^2/g3∥H1​∥2/g (Theorem 6.8).

Summary

  • Conservation is commutation with HHH: an observable keeps its statistics in every state exactly when it commutes with the Hamiltonian (Theorem 6.2).
  • A family of symmetries and its generator: the generator of a family of symmetries is conserved (Proposition 6.4).
  • Non-commuting symmetries force degeneracy (Theorem 6.5).
  • A small perturbation: for ∥H1∥≤g/4\norm{H_1}\le g/4∥H1​∥≤g/4, a level of rank ddd becomes ddd levels near ε\varepsilonε plus the eigenvalues of EH1EEH_1EEH1​E, up to 3∥H1∥2/g3\norm{H_1}^2/g3∥H1​∥2/g (Theorem 6.8).
  • Two levels: a coupling vvv keeps two levels at least 2∣v∣2\abs v2∣v∣ apart (Proposition 6.11).
