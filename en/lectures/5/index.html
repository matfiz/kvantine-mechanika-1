Lecture 5

Compatible and incompatible observables

What this lecture does

In Lecture 1, a reading along xxx undid a reading along zzz. This lecture finds when two readings do not disturb each other. Four conditions turn out to be the same: the order of two readings does not matter, a second reading never changes the first, an unrecorded reading never changes the statistics of the other, and the two observables commute. Commuting observables can be read in a single measurement, and enough of them pick out one state, whatever the system was before. For observables that do not commute, the spreads of their readings in one state obey a lower bound set by their commutator.

5.1 Two readings in turn

Let A=∑aa EaA=\sum_aa\,E_aA=∑a​aEa​ and B=∑bb GbB=\sum_bb\,G_bB=∑b​bGb​ be two observables of an nnn-level system. We write GbG_bGb​ for the projections of BBB, since the letters PPP and QQQ are kept for the momentum and the position of a particle. Suppose that AAA is measured and then, at once, BBB, with no evolution between the two readings. By Theorem 3.4 with the branch operator K=GbEaK=G_bE_aK=Gb​Ea​, and by the cyclicity of the trace and Gb2=GbG_b^2=G_bGb2​=Gb​, the readings “aaa, then bbb” occur with probability Prob⁡(a then b)=Tr⁡(GbEaρEaGb)=Tr⁡(ρ EaGbEa).\begin{equation} \label{eq:5:AB} \Prob(a\text{ then }b)=\Tr\bigl(G_bE_a\rho E_aG_b\bigr)=\Tr\bigl(\rho\,E_aG_bE_a\bigr). \end{equation}Prob(a then b)=Tr(Gb​Ea​ρEa​Gb​)=Tr(ρEa​Gb​Ea​).(5.1) In the other order, Prob⁡(b then a)=Tr⁡(ρ GbEaGb).\begin{equation} \label{eq:5:BA} \Prob(b\text{ then }a)=\Tr\bigl(\rho\,G_bE_aG_b\bigr). \end{equation}Prob(b then a)=Tr(ρGb​Ea​Gb​).(5.2)

It matters because the reading along xxx replaces the state, and with it the value along zzz that the first magnet had made certain.

5.2 Compatibility is commutation

Definition 5.1 (Compatible observables). Two observables AAA and BBB are compatible if, for every density matrix ρ\rhoρ and all readings aaa of AAA and bbb of BBB, Prob⁡(a then b)=Prob⁡(b then a).\Prob(a\text{ then }b)=\Prob(b\text{ then }a) .Prob(a then b)=Prob(b then a).

The definition asks for the equality in every state. §5.5 shows that agreement in one state does not establish compatibility. The next theorem gives a test that uses no state at all.

Theorem 5.2 (Compatible observables commute). For two observables A=∑aa EaA=\sum_aa\,E_aA=∑a​aEa​ and B=∑bb GbB=\sum_bb\,G_bB=∑b​bGb​ on Cn\C^nCn, the following are equivalent.

  1. [A,B]=0\comm AB=0[A,B]=0.

  2. AAA and BBB are compatible.

  3. A reading of BBB never changes the reading of AAA before it: for every density matrix, a measurement of AAA, then of BBB, then of AAA again, gives the same reading of AAA twice, with probability 111.

  4. A reading of AAA that is not recorded does not change the statistics of BBB: for every density matrix ρ\rhoρ and every bbb, Tr⁡(ρ′Gb)=Tr⁡(ρGb)\Tr(\rho'G_b)=\Tr(\rho G_b)Tr(ρ′Gb​)=Tr(ρGb​), where ρ′=∑aEaρEa\rho'=\sum_aE_a\rho E_aρ′=∑a​Ea​ρEa​ is the density matrix after the unrecorded reading (3.8).

The readings are made at once, with no evolution between them.

Proof. We prove that the first condition gives the other three, and that each of the other three gives the first.

The first gives the others. Let AB=BAAB=BAAB=BA. By Lemma 4.3, applied to the self-adjoint matrix AAA, the matrix BBB commutes with every EaE_aEa​. Applied again, to BBB and EaE_aEa​, the lemma shows that every EaE_aEa​ commutes with every GbG_bGb​. Then EaGbEa=GbEa=GbEaGbE_aG_bE_a=G_bE_a=G_bE_aG_bEa​Gb​Ea​=Gb​Ea​=Gb​Ea​Gb​, and the probabilities (5.1) and (5.2) are equal. For the third condition, the readings aaa, bbb, a′a'a′ have the branch operator Ea′GbEa=GbEa′EaE_{a'}G_bE_a=G_bE_{a'}E_aEa′​Gb​Ea​=Gb​Ea′​Ea​, which is 000 for a′≠aa'\ne aa′=a. So the second reading of AAA is aaa with probability 111. For the fourth, by the cyclicity of the trace, EaGb=GbEaE_aG_b=G_bE_aEa​Gb​=Gb​Ea​ and ∑aEa=11\sum_aE_a=\Id∑a​Ea​=11, Tr⁡(ρ′Gb)=Tr⁡(ρ∑aEaGbEa)=Tr⁡(ρ Gb∑aEa)=Tr⁡(ρGb).\Tr(\rho'G_b)=\Tr\Bigl(\rho\sum_aE_aG_bE_a\Bigr)=\Tr\Bigl(\rho\,G_b\sum_aE_a\Bigr) =\Tr(\rho G_b) .Tr(ρ′Gb​)=Tr(ρa∑​Ea​Gb​Ea​)=Tr(ρGb​a∑​Ea​)=Tr(ρGb​).

The second gives the first. Fix aaa and bbb, and put M=EaGbEa−GbEaGbM=E_aG_bE_a-G_bE_aG_bM=Ea​Gb​Ea​−Gb​Ea​Gb​, a self-adjoint matrix. The third gives the first. 5.3 Reading two observables at once

Compatible observables can be read together, by one measurement.

Proposition 5.3 (A joint reading). Let A=∑aa EaA=\sum_aa\,E_aA=∑a​aEa​ and B=∑bb GbB=\sum_bb\,G_bB=∑b​bGb​ commute. The matrices EaGbE_aG_bEa​Gb​ are orthogonal projections with ∑a,bEaGb=11\sum_{a,b}E_aG_b=\Id∑a,b​Ea​Gb​=11. These are the probabilities and the states of a single ideal measurement whose projections are the nonzero EaGbE_aG_bEa​Gb​.

Proof. By the proof of Theorem 5.2, EaGb=GbEaE_aG_b=G_bE_aEa​Gb​=Gb​Ea​. So (EaGb)†=GbEa=EaGb\adj{(E_aG_b)}=G_bE_a=E_aG_b(Ea​Gb​)†=Gb​Ea​=Ea​Gb​ and (EaGb)2=Ea2Gb2=EaGb(E_aG_b)^2=E_a^2G_b^2=E_aG_b(Ea​Gb​)2=Ea2​Gb2​=Ea​Gb​, and ∑a,bEaGb=(∑aEa)(∑bGb)=11\sum_{a,b}E_aG_b=\bigl(\sum_aE_a\bigr)\bigl(\sum_bG_b\bigr)=\Id∑a,b​Ea​Gb​=(∑a​Ea​)(∑b​Gb​)=11. For example, a measurement of an observable ∑a,bcab EaGb\sum_{a,b}c_{ab}\,E_aG_b∑a,b​cab​Ea​Gb​ with distinct real numbers cabc_{ab}cab​ is a single measurement with these projections. When every joint projection has rank 111, a joint reading fixes the state.

By Proposition 5.3, applied in turn, the products are projections. 5.5 What commutation does not say

The shared eigenvector of Example 5.5 is a warning. A common eigenvector does not make two observables compatible. Compatibility asks for the equality of Definition 5.1 in every state, and e0e_0e0​ is one state. In the same way, a state may give certain readings of two incompatible observables, as e0e_0e0​ does for ZZZ and XXX. Compatibility is a property of two observables, not of an observable and a state.

For a qubit, the readings along two axes obey an exact bound. By Born’s rule on the ball (§3.4), a reading along m\mvecm gives ±1\pm1±1 with probability 12(1±m⋅r)\tfrac12(1\pm\mvec\cdot\blochv)21​(1±m⋅r), so the difference of the two probabilities is m⋅r\mvec\cdot\blochvm⋅r.

So a state whose reading along zzz is certain, rz=±1r_z=\pm1rz​=±1, gives ±1\pm1±1 along xxx with probability 12\tfrac1221​ each.

Theorem 5.2 is about readings made at once. If the system evolves between them, a reading of AAA at one time and of AAA again later can disagree even with no reading in between. Whether a reading survives the evolution is the question of Lecture 6.

5.6 The spreads of two readings

An ideal measurement of AAA on many systems prepared in the state ρ\rhoρ gives readings that scatter about their mean ⟨A⟩=Tr⁡(ρA)\langle A\rangle=\Tr(\rho A)⟨A⟩=Tr(ρA). Compatible: AAA and BBB give the same probabilities in either order, for every state, if and only if [A,B]=0\comm AB=0[A,B]=0. Summary

  • Compatible observables commute: the order of two readings never matters, a second reading never changes the first, and an unrecorded reading never changes the statistics of the other, exactly when the two observables commute (Theorem 5.2).
  • Readings along zzz and xxx: on a qubit, rz2+rx2≤1r_z^2+r_x^2\le1rz2​+rx2​≤1 (Proposition 5.6).
  • A joint reading: commuting observables are read by a single measurement, and a complete set of them leaves one ray (Proposition 5.3, Definition 5.4).
  • The uncertainty relation: in every state, ΔA ΔB≥12∣Tr⁡(ρ[A,B])∣\Delta A\,\Delta B\ge\tfrac12\abs{\Tr(\rho\comm AB)}ΔAΔB≥21​∣Tr(ρ[A,B])∣ (Theorem 5.7).
