About this site

The companion to Quantum Mechanics I — finite systems.

What is here

14 lectures released so far, with 15 definitions, 67 numbered results, 67 proofs, 20 worked examples and 21 figures. Everything is generated from the same LaTeX source as the printed notes: a theorem here is the same object, with the same number, as the theorem in the PDF, and every theorem, equation and figure number on the site is checked against the typeset volume before the site is built.

This is a first course in quantum mechanics, for finite-dimensional systems. For each system it answers the same four questions: what are its states, what are its observables, how does it evolve, and what does a measurement give? The answers start from postulates, and in finite dimension everything that follows from them can be proved. It starts with the qubit, goes on to systems with n levels, and ends with several systems together.

It is written for reading alone, by students who have taken Linear Algebra for Physics and know no quantum mechanics. Each lecture is a unit of about 90 minutes of reading, and each of its sections ends in a self-check. Every lecture has an exercise sheet. Quantum Mechanics II: Continuous Systems and III: Foundations build on this course.

How much is proved

The notes are honest about rigour. A result with no tag is proved in full. A result tagged sketched is given with its central idea; one tagged cited is used, and its proof lives in the references. Every formula, sign and number the notes print is checked by a computer-algebra script before it is printed.

Questions and solutions

An exercise is a question, and a solution is the full worked method. The exercise sheets on this site, and their PDFs, are the questions-only edition.

They are not merely hidden here. Every solution is deleted from the source text before it reaches the converter, the public sheet PDFs are recompiled as the questions-only variant rather than copied from the repository, and the finished site and every PDF in it are searched for solution material before anything can be published.

Found a mistake?

Please report it to the course's author. Corrections during a course's first run are genuinely valuable: each is fixed at source and republished.

Colophon

Built as a static site from LaTeX. Pandoc preserves the notes' own definition, theorem, example and remark structure; the printed numbering and every cross-reference are recovered from the source and checked against the typeset volume; KaTeX renders the mathematics at build time with the course's own notation macros, translated automatically from the style file the PDFs load; the diagrams are the same TikZ drawings as in the PDF and the data figures the same generated plots, converted to SVG. The notes are set in Charter, the face the printed notes use. No tracking, no cookies, no third-party requests.
